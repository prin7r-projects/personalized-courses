# 08 · Marketing strategy

## Positioning (one sentence)

> A private tutor's reading list — written for one student, dripped weekly, graded against a rubric that reader saw at intake.

The core repositioning move is from _platform_ ("a place to learn") to _editorial artifact_ ("a syllabus made for you"). Reading List is closer in spirit to a small literary press than to Coursera.

## Messaging hierarchy

```
H1 (the hero)        →  "A syllabus of one. Built around the thing you actually want to learn."
H2 (the lede)        →  "Tell us your goal in one sentence and your current level in two.
                         Reading List answers with a personalized course — readings, videos,
                         exercises, evaluations — generated for you and dripped at a pace
                         that respects how an adult actually studies."
H3 (the proof)       →  the syllabus-of-one card on the right of the hero
H4 (the arrangement) →  "How a course is made for you" — 4 steps, plain language
H5 (the worked ex.)  →  "Ship a Rust CLI in 30 days" sample syllabus
H6 (the price)       →  "Buy a course or keep one going."
H7 (the voices)      →  3 paraphrased testimonials in editorial frame
H8 (the FAQ)         →  6 questions, answered straight
H9 (the desk)        →  a footer that points at one mailbox per audience
```

## Content pillars (12-month editorial calendar)

The studio's marketing is a content-marketing engine that produces useful artefacts. Eight pieces / quarter.

1. **A public syllabus per quarter.** A complete 4-module syllabus with named readings, exercises, rubric, and a rationale for the order — published under CC-BY. Each one anchors HN / subreddit posts.
2. **A "what we cut from the syllabus and why" essay per month.** The interesting decisions in personalized pedagogy are the cuts. We document them. ~1,200 words each.
3. **A monthly "voices" post.** One graduate's intake, one of their evaluations, and a reflection — with permission. Real names withheld.
4. **A quarterly "rubric library" release.** We publish a few topic-family rubrics (technical writing, language proficiency, mathematical reasoning) as a reference. Free to use.
5. **A monthly tutor-affiliate spotlight.** A working tutor we work with describes how their week looks now versus before. (Wave 3.)

## What we don't do (and won't)

- **No "AI-powered learning" marketing.** The phrase is exhausted. We don't open with the LLM. We open with the syllabus.
- **No certificate marketing.** No badges, no logos to put on LinkedIn. The artifact you ship _is_ the credential.
- **No cohort countdowns.** No "join now, only 24 hours left!". The drip is asynchronous; pressure tactics betray the brand.
- **No Lorem-ipsum case studies.** Every quoted student is a real graduate with permission.
- **No re-engagement email guilt-tripping.** If you cancel, you cancel. We send one "would you like to come back?" email at month +6, never sooner.

## SEO posture

We do not write for Google. We write for HN, Twitter, and forwarded Telegram links. SEO is a happy accident: long-form essays on pedagogy and personalized learning rank organically over 12–18 months. We don't spend on SEO tooling beyond a basic sitemap and `og:image` for each article.

## Brand consistency rules

The voice is editorial — first principle of every marketing decision.

- **Specificity over scope.** "Klabnik & Nichols ch. 4" not "Rust resources".
- **Restraint over enthusiasm.** Never "supercharge", "revolutionize", "10x". The studio is a small press, not a venture-backed platform.
- **Italics are a tool, not decoration.** One italic phrase per paragraph at most.
- **Marginalia is one sentence per section.** A handwritten margin note in marginalia-red, never two in the same section.
- **Photography is functional, not aspirational.** No stock laptop-on-desk shots. Where imagery is used, it is either the syllabus card itself or a paper-textured background block.

## Measurement

- **Primary KPI**: paid course completions (not sign-ups, not "enrolments"). Wave 3 will publish a monthly completion-rate badge on the home page (if it's not above 60%, we don't show it).
- **Secondary KPI**: time-to-first-syllabus (from intake to locked syllabus). Target: 24 hours for self-serve, 48 hours for cohorts.
- **Tertiary KPI**: refund rate. Target: <8% of single-course sales, <3% of subscription cancellations.
- **Vanity metrics we don't track**: page views, time on site, social-follower count.
