We **do not** use:

- Google Ads on broad terms ("learn Rust", "online course"). The CPC is set by Coursera / Udemy budgets. We don't outbid them and we don't want to.

- LinkedIn cold outreach. Mara doesn't want a DM from a sales rep.
- Mailing lists we didn't earn. Every newsletter we touch is double-opt-in.

## Channel-by-channel notes

### 1. Hacker News + technical subreddits

**Tactic.** Once per quarter, the studio publishes a _public_ syllabus on the website (CC-BY) — e.g. "Ship a Rust CLI in 30 days" with all named readings, exercises, and rubrics open. The post is signed by the lead pedagogue, references the Reading List home page once at the bottom, and otherwise behaves like a useful artifact. The HN front page is downstream of useful artifacts.

**Conversion model.** ~3% of HN visitors land on the home page; ~5% of those click a pricing tier; ~30% of those complete a NOWPayments invoice. ≈ 5 paying buyers per 10 K HN readers.

**Why this fits.** HN punishes self-promotion. It rewards craft. Reading List's brand voice _is_ the marketing — there is no other tonal mode.

### 2. Private Telegram engineering chats

**Tactic.** The studio doesn't post in these chats — it earns mentions by being something a member would forward. Forward velocity is the actual KPI.

**Levers.** Quote-shareable lines in the landing copy ("a syllabus of one"; "a course that finishes with you, not on you"). One-image previews of the syllabus card that look good in a Telegram message preview.

**Why this fits.** The audience is risk-averse on courses but values peer recommendations. Trust transfers cleanly.

### 3. Cohort-of-five via engineering managers

**Tactic.** The "For teams · for tutors" block on the landing is the entire top-of-funnel. Inbound emails go to `cohorts@personalized-courses.prin7r.com` and get a same-day reply with a one-page agreement. The volume is low (4–8 inbound a quarter, 1–2 closed) but the LTV is high ($30–35/seat × 5 seats × 12 months = $1,800–2,100 ACV).

**Why this fits.** Hannah does not want a sales process. She wants to know we exist, that the agreement is short, that her engineers will not file complaints. The studio answers all three concerns in 24 hours.

### 4. Tutor-affiliate program (Lola pattern)

**Tactic.** A working private tutor (language, music, mathematics, oral coaching, audition prep) signs up as a "studio tutor". They bring their existing client list. The studio runs the syllabus + drip + grading layer. The tutor takes 70% of the coached-tier MRR; the studio takes 30% and provides the platform.

**Why this fits.** Tutors are the _sympathetic_ middlemen of education. They want their students to succeed, they want a structured artifact to point at, and they don't want to build an LMS. We are the LMS they would have built.

### 5. Engineering newsletter placements

**Tactic.** One paid placement per quarter in a curated technical newsletter (Bytes, TLDR, Programmer Brain, Pointer, Bytesized News). Copy is editorial — a 100-word block describing one concrete course we're running, with the syllabus URL.

**Why this fits.** These newsletters have audiences with high "I will pay for craft" tendency. Their subscribers are exactly the audience we want.

**Cost target.** $400–800 per placement. Conversion: ~25–40 buyers per placement.

### 6. Twitter / X engineering threads

**Tactic.** The studio's lead pedagogue posts long-form threads (10–25 tweets) about pedagogy and self-directed learning. Threads anchor to goals ("how to read the Rust book without getting stuck at chapter 5") and link to a public syllabus draft. The studio account doesn't tweet about generic AI hype.

**Why this fits.** Slow burn — a year of consistent posting compounds. The audience overlaps perfectly with HN.

### 7. Direct (`tutor@`)

**Tactic.** The desk is staffed within ten hours, every day. Replies are written in the brand voice (editorial, specific, restrained). Inbound questions are answered with concrete sentences, not "happy to chat!" deflections. ~60% of replied conversations close.

**Why this fits.** Adults who hesitated in front of the pricing page need one round of human reassurance. The desk _is_ a sales channel — we just don't call it that.

## Anti-channels (and why)

- **Coursera marketplace partnership.** We sell into a different value frame; living on someone else's marketplace would erase the brand.
- **YouTube ads.** The audience is anti-streamlined-video. Wrong medium, wrong tone.
- **Reseller / agency programs.** We don't have the gross margin for 30% partner cuts (LLM costs eat it). The cohort-via-eng-manager channel achieves the same volume with better unit economics.
