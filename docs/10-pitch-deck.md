## Slide 1 — Title

**Curriculum7**

## Slide 2 — The problem

Self-directed adult learners have been failed by mass-market online learning.

- Coursera completion rates are <5%.
- Buyers have backlogs of unfinished courses they feel guilty about.
- The mass-market course is built for an _imagined average student_, not a real one.

The crisis is not access. The crisis is fit.

## Slide 3 — The buyer's reality

Mara, 34, backend engineer in Lisbon. Wants to ship a Rust CLI in 30 days.

- Has bought two Udemy courses, finished none.
- Started Klabnik & Nichols, lost the borrow checker by chapter five.
- Has 4–6 hours of weekly study time and a deadline.
- Is willing to pay; cannot find a course built for her.

We exist for Mara. The studio is sized to serve her.

## Slide 4 — The product

Curriculum7 takes one declared goal and one paragraph of context, and produces:

- A personalized syllabus on a single page (4 modules max).
- A weekly drip (Mondays 09:00 buyer's local time).
- One reading + one exercise + one self-evaluation per module.
- An end-of-course graded artifact the buyer keeps.

Three tiers: $149 single course · $39/mo subscription · $299/mo coach-augmented.

## Slide 5 — Why now

Three forces converged in 2025–2026:

- **LLMs got good at structured output.** GLM 5.1 Flash + Claude 4.7 reliably draft a syllabus that passes editorial review with light edits.
- **Citation infrastructure got open.** Crossref + OpenLibrary cover ~80% of the named readings we surface. We can verify before drip.
- **Adult learners got disenchanted.** The "Coursera certificate" trade has clearly failed. Buyers want craft; they have stopped expecting it from platforms.

## Slide 6 — The unit economics

Per-buyer view (single-course tier):

- Price: $149.
- LLM cost (syllabus draft + 4 module evaluations): $4–7.
- Editorial review (10 min of human time at $50/h): $8.
- Grading (machine, except coached tier): negligible.
- **Gross margin: ~89%.**

Standing subscription is 4× margin-positive vs. quarterly LLM cost. Coach-augmented is a revenue-share with the tutor pool — non-cash-intensive.

## Slide 7 — Distribution

We do not buy keyword search. We earn distribution by publishing useful artefacts.

- One public syllabus per quarter (CC-BY) — anchors HN / subreddit posts.
- One paid newsletter placement per quarter (Bytes, TLDR, Programmer Brain).
- Direct desk hours (`tutor@`) — answer email well, close 60% of replies.
- Tutor-affiliate program (Wave 3) — working tutors bring their existing clients.

## Slide 8 — Traction (90 days)

Targets for the first quarter (Wave 2 + Wave 3 onboarding):

- 50 paid single-course sales.
- 12 standing-subscription customers.
- 1 cohort (5 seats × $35 × 12 months).
- 1 active tutor-affiliate.
- Cumulative revenue: ~$13 K. Cumulative spend: ~$15.5 K.

Break-even target: month 6 cumulative.
